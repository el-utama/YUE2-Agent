# Lyric Style Guide — Writing Human, Not AI

> Purpose: give the lyricist agent concrete, real-world patterns so output sounds like a person who
> grew up on these songs, not a language model. Each genre lists current global hit-makers, 2–3
> reference tracks, and the craft mechanics behind them.
>
> Copyright note: all lyrics below are **brief illustrative fragments (a line or phrase)** used for
> analysis only, not reproductions of full songs. Prefer paraphrase when drafting; never ship a full
> verse/chorus lifted from a real song.

---

## Global Anti-AI Checklist (read before every draft)

**Ban these clichés outright (generic AI filler):**
`heart of gold` · `dance in the rain` · `stars align` · `whispers in the dark` · `set me free` ·
`chasing dreams` · `painted skies` · `broken wings` · `light up the night` · `diamond in the rough` ·
`against all odds` · `shattered pieces` · `ocean of tears` · `eternal flame` · `fire/desire/higher`
rhyme trio · `only you` · `forever and a day` · `lost in your eyes` · `mysterious as the night`.

**Craft rules that make it human:**
1. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the street, the brand, the weather, the food, the body sensation.
   "Cheap sunglasses, gas-station coffee" beats "endless summer of love."
2. **Colloquial contractions & elision.** gonna, wanna, ain't, 'til, 'em, y'all, dunno, tryna,
   kinda, outta. Real speech drops and slurs syllables.
3. **Imperfect / slant rhymes.** time/mind, home/alone, orange/sporange, fire/desire avoided.
   Allow assonance and multisyllabic rhyme (e.g., "American" / "barely an").
4. **Break perfect symmetry.** Do not write 8 identical syllables × 8 lines. Mix 3-word lines with
   12-word lines; vary the rhyme scheme (AABB, ABAB, ABCB, unrhymed).
5. **Specific place/time anchors.** "Tuesday, 3 a.m.", "Route 9", "the corner store", "your mom's
   old Camry", "last November."
6. **Fragments & mid-sentence starts.** Open on a conjunction ("And I...", "But you..."), a name, or
   a half-thought. Conversation doesn't begin with a topic sentence.
7. **Show, don't explain emotion.** Physical action > stated feeling ("you bit your lip" > "you were
   nervous").
8. **No tidy resolution.** End a verse/bridge on an unresolved image or a turn, not a moral summary.
9. **Real names & mundane nouns.** Brand names, cities, jobs, foods ground the fantasy.
10. **One weird, specific detail per verse** is worth more than ten generic poetic ones.

**Test:** if a line could appear in a greeting-card ad, cut it.

## 1. Pop

**Artists:** Taylor Swift, Ariana Grande, Billie Eilish, Dua Lipa, The Weeknd, Olivia Rodrigo,
Sabrina Carpenter, Chappell Roan.

- **Taylor Swift — "Anti-Hero" (2022)** — Excerpt: *"It's me, hi, I'm the problem, it's me."*
  Rhyme: conversational AABB with internal echo. Imagery: neurosis, midnight, 30-something dread,
  mundane self-loathing. Vocab: plain, confessional. Slang: casual elision. Line length: short,
  punchy, speech-like. Hook: title as ironic mantra + repetition. Pacing: verse→pre→chorus, tight,
  chorus lands fast. Tone: wry, self-lacerating. AI-isms to avoid: "demons in my head", "darkness
  consumes me."

- **Olivia Rodrigo — "drivers license" (2021)** — Excerpt: *"And I know we weren't perfect."*
  Rhyme: loose ABAB, near-rhyme "you / blue." Imagery: suburbs, driving alone, a specific girl's
  name, dusk. Vocab: teenage-plain. Slang: natural, no forced swagger. Line length: long, breathless
  run-ons. Hook: title-phrase repeated as a question of grief. Pacing: slow build, chorus swells,
  bridge detonates. Tone: wounded, obsessive. AI-isms: "tears like rain", "you broke me in two."

- **Dua Lipa — "Levitating" (2020)** — Excerpt: *"You want me, I want you, baby."*
  Rhyme: playful ABAB, double entendre. Imagery: sugar, disco, space, sweetness. Vocab: light,
  flirty. Slang: bubblegum modern. Line length: bouncy, symmetrical-ish (on purpose, it's disco).
  Hook: one-word title-hook "levitating" as a lift. Pacing: steady four-on-floor energy, minimal
  bridge. Tone: euphoric, coy. AI-isms: "cosmic love", "written in the stars."

**Do:** confessional specificity; let the chorus be a repeatable mantra; use one unusual concrete noun
per verse; keep contractions loose and speech-like.
**Don't:** stack abstractions; write a chorus that explains the feeling instead of naming the scene;
rhyme "fire/desire/higher"; make every line the same length.

## 2. Rock

**Artists:** Arctic Monkeys, Foo Fighters, Måneskin, Paramore, The Black Keys, Royal Blood,
Turnstile, IDLES.

- **Arctic Monkeys — "Do I Wanna Know?" (2013)** — Excerpt: *"Crawling back to you."*
  Rhyme: tight slant rhyme, repetition-driven. Imagery: late-night texting, alcohol, a rug-burn
  intimacy, a specific "you." Vocab: streetwise British, dry. Slang: "crawling," "summat"-adjacent
  elision. Line length: short, riff-locked, ragged. Hook: the question-hook repeated, riff as hook.
  Pacing: verses ride a groove; chorus is an eruption not a lift. Tone: smug, yearning, cool.
  AI-isms: "burning desire", "lost in the crowd."

- **Foo Fighters — "Everlong" (1997)** — Excerpt: *"And I wonder / when I sing along with you."*
  Rhyme: ABAB with long vowels, echo repeats. Imagery: breath, touch, dying-and-waking, static.
  Vocab: direct, urgent. Slang: minimal. Line length: mid-long, gospelly build. Hook: wordless "oh"
  refrain as the hook. Pacing: dynamic quiet-loud; chorus explodes. Tone: desperate tenderness.
  AI-isms: "eternal flame", "forever you and I."

- **Måneskin — "Beggin'" (2021 cover / 2017)** — Excerpt: *"I'm beggin', beggin' you."*
  Rhyme: AABB gospel-pop with call-response. Imagery: pleading, religious supplication, sweat.
  Vocab: soul-showman English, Italian-band swagger. Slang: emphatic repetition. Line length: long
  belted lines. Hook: title-as-chant, gospel "oohs." Pacing: slow-burn intro → full-band belted
  chorus. Tone: theatrical desperation. AI-isms: "on my knees for you" (unless earned), "queen of
  my heart."

**Do:** ride the groove; use repetition as an instrument; let grit and breath into the vocal line;
one raunchy/specific image beats ten poetic ones.
**Don't:** make the chorus a tidy summary; clean up the edges; use arena-rock filler ("we are the
young", "rise up").

## 3. Hip-Hop / Rap

**Artists:** Kendrick Lamar, Drake, J. Cole, Travis Scott, Doja Cat, Tyler, the Creator,
Nicki Minaj, 21 Savage.

- **Kendrick Lamar — "HUMBLE." (2017)** — Excerpt: *"Sit down, be humble."*
  Rhyme: dense multisyllabic + internal rhyme, punchline-driven. Imagery: posture, power, religious
  imagery turned cocky, body control. Vocab: elevated slang + church diction. Slang: heavy, layered,
  double meanings. Line length: clipped staccato + long flex runs. Hook: command-chant hook. Pacing:
  bouncy trap beat, verse is a boxing match. Tone: cocky, moralizing, funny. AI-isms: "grind till
  you shine", "started from the bottom" (unless referencing).

- **J. Cole — "No Role Modelz" (2014)** — Excerpt: *"I wanna be the one to make you proud."*
  Rhyme: relaxed storytelling couplets + assonance. Imagery: suburbia, absent fathers, a specific
  mama, first apartment. Vocab: plain-spoken, diary-confessional. Slang: natural Southern-inflected.
  Line length: conversational long bars. Hook: reflective refrain over sample. Pacing: linear
  narrative, no big drop — thought flows. Tone: earnest, wounded, wise. AI-isms: "hustle hard",
  "never fold."

- **Doja Cat — "Paint the Town Red" (2023)** — Excerpt: *"I'm a [expletive] — you already know."*
  (fragment used for attitude, not reproduced.) Rhyme: boastful callouts, sampled hook. Imagery:
  flame, gossip, enemies, theatrical villainy. Vocab: brash, self-aware. Slang: heavy internet-era.
  Line length: punchy flexes between sample. Hook: repurposed sample as the hook. Pacing: bouncy,
  meme-literate. Tone: unbothered, menacing-funny. AI-isms: "haters gonna hate", generic flex lists.

**Do:** write in your own voice with a distinct persona; land punchlines; let syntax breathe (long
bars, short bars); use concrete status symbols and specific feuds.
**Don't:** write generic "grind" motivation; rhyme "money/honey"; flatten every line into the same
cadence; over-explain the punchline.

## Quick Reference — Section Pacing & Hook Shapes

| Genre | Hook shape | Typical pacing |
|---|---|---|
| Pop | Title as mantra, repeatable | verse→pre→chorus, fast |
| Rock | Riff + repeated line | quiet-loud dynamics |
| Hip-Hop | Punchline / chant | bar-driven, hook repeats |
| R&B/Soul | Confession or title-chant | slow build → drop |
| Country | Title simile / scene | linear, conversational |
| Electronic | Single repeated phrase | loop → textured build |
| Latin | Title-chant / call-answer | hook-first, dembow groove |
| K-Pop | Short chant + dance break | maximal, hook-heavy |
| Afrobeats | Title-chant + reply | groove-first, hook early |
| Jazz/Blues | Standards refrain | jazz form, rubato |

**Final rule for the lyricist:** write like someone with a specific memory, not a summary of a
genre. One real detail beats ten poetic ones. When in doubt, cut the abstract line and keep the
concrete one.
